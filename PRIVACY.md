# Exo Launcher privacy statement

**Exo Launcher is local-first. It has no Exo account, no ads, no analytics and no telemetry.**

Your library, installs, updates, game launches and settings work offline. Exo has no server of its own that they depend on.

## What stays on this PC

Exo keeps its data in `%LocalAppData%\ExoLauncher`. This includes:

- settings (`settings.json`, which also holds your Library folders), the library cache, playtime Exo recorded, hours Xbox reported (`reported-hours.json`: game titles and minutes only), window position, cover art and thumbnail caches, achievement cache, and upscaler backups;
- copies of custom game covers you choose. They are stored locally and never uploaded;
- your Store wishlist (`store/wishlist-v2.json`) and the last sale price Exo told you about, the Store's saved pages (`store/home-v5.json`, Steam's public facts about the games it showed in `store/items-v2.json`, tag names in `store/tags-v1.json`), and your Exo Mouse profiles (`mouse/profiles.json`);
- the parts Exo fetches when it installs: the store tools (Legendary, gogdl, Nile) and their data, DeepFilterNet for noise suppression, and NVIDIA's TitaNet and Parakeet models for Only My Voice and dictation (in `tools`, `voice` and `models`; uninstalling Exo removes them), plus the browser data from the Epic and GOG sign-in windows;
- Exo Shaders, the shader engine behind PC > Graphics > Shaders, keeps its compile records, recordings and settings in `%LOCALAPPDATA%\SCSKiller` (the same folder SCSKiller uses). Exo passes it your installed games' names and folders so it can find their shaders; nothing leaves your PC (see **Exo Shaders** under **Other network requests** for the files it may download). It runs only when it has work, and Exo keeps in `shaders-engine.json` what it saw last time (your graphics driver's version, short hashes of your installed games and their programs, and when it ran) so it starts only when something changed;
- Exo Capture, the add-on behind the Capture button, saves the instant replays, recordings and snips you ask for in your captures folder (`Videos\Exo Captures` unless you choose another), and keeps its settings, thumbnails and which game each capture belongs to in `capture` under Exo's data folder. It captures the game's window or the screen through Windows; only when Windows gets no picture from a game (exclusive full screen) does it load OBS Studio's game capture hook into that game to copy its frames, never into Riot games or while FACEIT or ESEA runs. Every replay and recording has four sound tracks: the mix, the game (on the desktop, everything you hear except Discord), Discord voice while Discord runs, and your default microphone. There is no setting to leave one out; mute a track in your player or editor instead. While instant replay or a recording runs, your microphone is in use and Windows shows its microphone indicator. Instant replay holds the last minutes in memory, by default only while a game is running, and writes nothing until you save. Captures never leave your PC by themselves: Send to Discord copies the file and opens the Discord room for you to paste it into a chat. A storage limit or age you set sends the oldest captures to the Recycle Bin; favorites are always kept. A capture you delete goes to the Recycle Bin too;
- the browser profile used for YouTube Music and Apple Music;
- logs (see **Logs** below).

**Dictation.** The mic in Exo's text boxes (Settings > General > Dictation) uses your microphone only from when you press it until you press Stop, at most a minute, and Windows shows its microphone indicator. What you say is turned into text on this PC by NVIDIA's Parakeet model and goes into the text box. The sound stays in memory until then; it is never saved, logged or sent anywhere.

To find your games, Exo reads local folders, store manifests, registry keys and data saved by store clients on this PC. For hours, it also reads GOG Galaxy 2.0's database (`galaxy-2.0.db`, read-only) for the time Galaxy holds for games on the other platforms you connected to it. Exo does not upload your library, install folders, launch settings, playtime or achievements. Some services see a game title or store id when Exo looks up artwork or hours for it; they are listed under **Other network requests**.

To free memory, Exo reads the process list on this PC (names, parent processes, and the command line of a Battle.net process to tell its renderers apart) to find the browser helpers of Steam, the Epic Games Launcher, the EA app, Ubisoft Connect, GOG Galaxy and Battle.net, and lowers their memory use while they are hidden. It reads nothing inside them, keeps nothing and sends nothing.

**Keys and sign-ins stay local.** Optional keys you paste (Steam Web API, itch.io, SteamGridDB, OpenXBL) and your saved Riot ID are encrypted with Windows (DPAPI) for your Windows user. They are not written to `settings.json`, not shown back in the app, and only sent to the service they belong to (see **Other network requests**). Other programs running as the same Windows user can still get at them.

Store sign-ins stay with the official tools:

- Epic sign-in goes to Legendary.
- GOG sign-in goes to gogdl.
- Amazon sign-in goes to Nile.

Exo opens the store's own sign-in page, passes the result to that tool, and does not keep its own copy of the token.

**Accounts in Settings > Accounts and keys.** To show who each store is signed in as, Exo reads the display names in Steam's `config\loginusers.vdf`, Legendary's `user.json`, Nile's `user.json`, and the Epic Games Launcher's account id in the registry (to tell whether it uses a different account). Only display names reach the page; account names, ids and tokens are never logged. Switch account on Steam does what Steam's own account picker does: it closes Steam, marks that account as the one to sign in to in `loginusers.vdf` and the registry (`AutoLoginUser`, `RememberPassword`), and starts Steam again. Exo never sees a Steam password and never touches `config.vdf`. Epic accounts you add for their hours each get their own Legendary folder in `%LocalAppData%\ExoLauncher\epic-accounts`, and the last Epic playtime of each account signed in on this PC is kept in `epic-playtime.json` under a one-way tag. With **Count hours from all my accounts** on, Exo adds up the hours each Steam account on this PC and each of those Epic accounts recorded; nothing is sent anywhere new.

**Store settings Exo changes.** Exo turns the Steam overlay off for Steam games (Settings > Accounts and keys > Steam overlay turns it back on, and Properties does it for one game), and **Notifications and sounds** turns off Steam's and Epic's own pop-ups and sounds. Exo writes these switches into each Steam account's `localconfig.vdf` and `sharedconfig.vdf` and into Epic's own settings, on this PC, while those apps are closed; nothing else in those files changes. When you press Play on a Steam game, Exo accepts that game's own EULA and Steam's default launch choice if Steam asks, never Steam's Subscriber Agreement or a purchase.

**When Steam's servers are down.** While Steam runs, Exo reads two of Steam's own logs on this PC every 15 seconds (`logs\connection_log.txt` and `logs\steamui_login.txt`: whether Steam's sign-in was answered, and Steam's own internet check) to tell you once when Steam can't reach its servers; nothing is sent anywhere and nothing from them is kept. If you choose Play offline, Exo closes Steam (never while a Steam game runs), turns on Steam's own offline mode for the account Steam signs in as (`WantsOfflineMode` and `SkipOfflineModeWarning` in `config\loginusers.vdf`, what Steam's Go Offline writes), and starts Steam again. It first keeps a copy of that file (`steam-offline-loginusers.vdf`) and the two values it had (`steam-offline.json`) in Exo's data folder, puts exactly those values back when Steam's servers answer again (or after you go online in Steam), and then deletes both. Nothing else in the file changes, and no password or token is read.

**Gifts.** To show gifts waiting for you in the Library, Exo opens Steam's and Epic's own gifts pages in a hidden browser with its own profile (`gifts-webview` in Exo's data folder) when you open the Library (at most every half hour) or refresh it, and every half hour while Exo is in front and you're not playing, and reads them; Accept and Decline make the same calls those pages make. Steam's website needs one sign-in there, on Steam's own page (Settings > Game stores > Steam gifts); it stays in that profile until you sign out there. Epic's store page is signed in with a one-time code from Exo's Epic sign-in. Gift names, senders and messages are shown, never saved, logged or sent anywhere else.

## Exo accounts (removed)

Exo 3.4 removed the optional Exo account. The app has no sign-in, sign-up, sign-out, handle, badges, export or delete screens any more, and **it never contacts the Exo account service (exo-id)**. Recent versions synced nothing to the account, so your library, playtime, settings and keys are already on this PC and keep working as before.

**On this PC.** The first time Exo 3.4 or later starts, it deletes what an earlier version saved for the account, in `%LocalAppData%\ExoLauncher` and `%AppData%\ExoLauncher`:

- `auth.bin`, the session token, encrypted with Windows (DPAPI), that kept you signed in, together with the account id, handle and email it held, plus any unfinished copy (`auth.bin.tmp`);
- the `online-cache` folder of saved account responses;
- `device-id`, the per-PC id very old versions sent with a library activity summary.

This is a local cleanup only: Exo does not sign out with the service or send it anything. It runs again on every start, so a copy restored from a backup is removed too. Exo writes one line to its log when it removes a saved sign-in.

**On the service.** If you created an account with an earlier version, the service still holds what it held before: your account id, display name, email, a salted password hash, session records (which may include an IP address and an app or browser identifier), and anything older versions stored, such as a handle, profile fields, a library activity summary, online status, preferences, friends or store links. Exo 3.4 does not add to it or change it. That data stays on the service (Cloudflare Workers with a Cloudflare D1 database) until the service is shut down and its data deleted. Until then, Exo 3.3.x can still download a copy (Settings > Exo account > Download) or delete the account (Settings > Exo account > Delete account); deleting removes the account and everything linked to it. Sessions end after 7 days, and the service removes expired ones every hour.

## Connected apps

Exo's Discord room and Music room talk to those services for you, with your own sign-in. Each service keeps its own data and privacy policy. What Exo reads from them (your messages, your library, what you played) is shown to you and not stored, logged or sent anywhere else.

### Discord

- **Exo's own Discord client.** The Discord room is Exo's own client; Exo does not run, install or change the Discord desktop app. You sign in by scanning a QR code with Discord's phone app, so Exo never sees your password. Exo keeps the sign-in on this PC, encrypted for your Windows account (`discord-client.bin` in Exo's data folder), and sends it only to Discord. Messages are shown, never saved or logged, and only channels your roles let you see are shown. Like Discord's app, Exo sends Discord your status and, only when your Discord account shares it (Activity Privacy), the name of the game you're playing. An invite link you click is sent to Discord to join that server. In a call, your microphone is used only while you're in it, and in Discord settings > Voice & Video only while its level meter shows (Windows shows its microphone indicator); its sound is cleaned of noise and echo on this PC (DeepFilterNet and WebRTC's echo canceller, or NVIDIA's noise removal on an RTX card once you download it); with Only My Voice, Exo learns your voice from a Voice Check you choose to use, keeps that voiceprint (192 numbers) on this PC, encrypted for your Windows account (`voice\my-voice.bin`), and compares voices with it only on this PC; Forget deletes it; encrypted end to end with Discord's DAVE protocol and sent only to Discord's voice server. Mentions and DMs show as Windows notifications with the message's text, as Discord's app does. Discord settings change your Discord account's own settings on Discord, as Discord's apps do; what Discord keeps per device (Accessibility, Streamer Mode, the camera, keybinds, sounds) stays on this PC. Your email and phone number are shown masked and never reach the page in full. Discord's sounds and the fonts of its display name styles are fetched once from discord.com and kept in Exo's data folder. The Shop shows Discord's catalog and the collectibles you own. Buying with a card already saved on your Discord account happens through Discord, which shows Exo only the card's brand, last four digits and expiry; Exo never sees or stores card numbers or your billing address, and anything else finishes in Discord's own shop in your browser. Keybinds read the keys you hold only while you're in a call, and never record or send them. Sign out removes the sign-in and ends it on Discord. Discord's terms do not allow third-party clients; that risk comes with signing in.

### Spotify

This is your installed desktop app, which plays the music hidden behind Exo. You sign in to Spotify on Spotify's own page in your default browser. When Exo starts Spotify:

- **Spotify.** Exo recolors it and hides ads, promos and install prompts by adding CSS to Spotify's local `xpui.spa` file, turns on two of Spotify's own preferences that hide home-page takeovers and promoted songs, and turns off its Windows notification on every new song. It turns on Spotify's "system media controls" preference so the mini player can follow playback. It also starts Spotify with a private debugging pipe that only Exo holds (no port another program could find), and uses it to hide Spotify's window buttons and to pause Canvas videos.

All of these changes stay on this PC. The mini player reads what is playing from Windows' media controls.

**Exo's Music room for Spotify** draws Spotify's pages itself. Through that same private pipe it asks the Spotify app for what the room shows (your library, home, search, playlists, albums, artists, podcasts, the queue, devices, recently played, lyrics of the playing song, your friends' activity, and your Jam) and passes on what you click (play, queue, like, add to or remove from a playlist, start, leave or end a Jam, remove someone from it). Spotify makes every one of these requests itself, with its own sign-in, exactly as when you click in Spotify; Exo never reads your token or cookies. Only small summaries (names, picture links, durations) reach Exo's window, and nothing is stored. A Jam's invite link reaches Exo only to be copied when you press Copy invite link. Album art, profile pictures and lyrics' covers load in Exo's window straight from Spotify's image servers (`*.scdn.co`, `*.spotifycdn.com`).

### Your mouse (Exo Mouse)

In the Devices room, your mouse's page is Exo Mouse, which is part of Exo's download and talks to your mouse directly over USB HID. It reads and writes your mouse's own settings (such as DPI, polling rate, buttons, lighting, battery level and onboard profiles). That data stays on this PC: profiles you save in Exo Mouse (a name, the settings to put back and the games they are for) are kept in Exo's data folder on this PC (`mouse\profiles.json`), nothing is sent anywhere, and Exo needs no OpenMouse app, vendor software or driver for it. To show a picture of your mouse, Exo Mouse loads its product render from OpenMouse's image server (img.openmouse.app), and the Devices room's overview shows that same picture on your mouse's card; the request names the model's image file, and the image is then kept in Exo's browser cache. Your keyboard, controller and headphones are shown with their maker's own product picture. Exo picks it from a list of links built into Exo, by what Windows and the device report (its name, a keyboard's layout, a DualSense's body color) or the headphones you picked in Lucid, and requests only that image file from the maker's image server (see **Device pictures** below). Nothing about you or this PC goes with the request, and no cookies; as when you open the maker's product page, the image server sees which product's picture was asked for. Exo fits the picture to the Devices room's frame and keeps it on this PC (`device-images` in Exo's data folder), so each picture is fetched once. A device with no picture in Exo's list is drawn by Exo itself.

Earlier versions of Exo answered the OpenMouse web panel through a bridge of their own and could stop the OpenMouse Bridge app to do it. That bridge is gone. The first time this version starts, if Exo had stopped the OpenMouse Bridge app, Exo puts its start with Windows back exactly as it was and writes the profiles Exo kept into the app's own settings file, then deletes Exo's copy. It does not start the app.

### YouTube Music

YouTube Music runs inside Exo as a web page (Microsoft Edge WebView2), using a browser profile stored under `%LocalAppData%\ExoLauncher`. You sign in to Google there, and Google handles that data.

Exo's Music room draws YouTube Music's pages itself and keeps that page out of sight behind it (it shows only so you can sign in). Exo asks the page for what the room shows (home, search, your library, playlists, albums, artists, the queue, lyrics) and passes on what you click (play, like, shuffle, repeat); the page makes every request itself with your Google sign-in, as when you click in it, and Exo never reads its cookies or tokens. Only small summaries reach Exo's window, and nothing is stored. Covers load in Exo's window straight from Google's image servers (`*.googleusercontent.com`, `i.ytimg.com`).

### Apple Music

Apple Music runs inside Exo as Apple's own web player (music.apple.com) in the same browser profile. You sign in to Apple there, in Apple's own sign-in window, and Apple handles that data. Exo's Music room draws Apple Music's pages itself and keeps that page out of sight behind it (it shows only so you can sign in). Exo asks the page's own Apple Music player (MusicKit) for what the room shows (home, recently played, search, your library, playlists, albums, artists, the queue, lyrics of the playing song) and passes on what you click (play, add to the queue, favorite, add to or remove from your library or a playlist, shuffle, repeat, volume). The page makes every request itself, with Apple's own developer token and your sign-in; Exo never reads, stores or copies those tokens or its cookies. Music plays inside Apple's page, protected as Apple protects it; Exo never handles the audio. Only small summaries (names, picture links, durations) reach Exo's window, and nothing is stored. Covers load in Exo's window straight from Apple's image servers (`*.mzstatic.com`). The Apple Music app for Windows is no longer used.

Exo adds these browser extensions to that profile:

- **uBlock Origin Lite** blocks ads and trackers with the filter lists built into it, and downloads no lists of its own.
- **SponsorBlock** asks sponsor.ajay.app about the video that is playing.
- **Better Lyrics** fetches lyrics from outside lyrics services.

These extensions have their own privacy policies. A music page that asks for your microphone, camera, clipboard or notifications asks you first; Settings > Accounts and keys > Music sets YouTube Music's notifications.

## Logs

Exo writes a diagnostic log to `%LocalAppData%\ExoLauncher\logs\app.log`. It holds up to about 2 MB, plus one older file. The log can include:

- game titles and folder names;
- app and store tool messages;
- errors from the app's interface;
- crash details.

Before writing, Exo removes things that look like tokens, passwords, keys, account or store ids, and email addresses. **Logs never leave your PC.** Exo does not upload them. **Open logs**, offered when part of Exo stops, only opens the folder. If you share a log for support, check it first.

## What Exo Launcher does not do

- No ads, analytics, telemetry or crash-reporting service. Crashes are written to the local log only.
- No Exo account, and no online dependency for your library or game actions.
- No library activity, playtime or per-PC id sent anywhere. Your online status, and your current game when your Discord account shares it, go only to Discord (see **Discord**).
- No uploading of your library, install folders, playtime history or store sign-ins.
- No Exo friends list, profiles, handles, badges, chat or settings sync. The friends, profiles and chat in the Discord room are Discord's own.
- No kernel driver, anti-cheat bypass, or silent approval of Windows security prompts.
- The clipboard is read only when you open **Activate key**, to spot a game key, and only on this PC. Exo then opens the store's own redeem page (for Epic it copies the key for you to paste); it does not send the key anywhere itself. Exo Capture writes to the clipboard when you snip or press Copy or Send to Discord, and never reads it. In Exo's own Discord client, Copy Image puts the picture on the clipboard and Save Image saves it in your Downloads folder.
